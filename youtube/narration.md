## 1. Before anything arrives — 0:30–1:10
*Card: `01` · Before anything arrives*
*≈ 88 words · ≈ 36 s*

> The work starts before the van does.
>
> You tell us what's coming: which products, how many of each, and roughly when.
>
> That one message settles half the problems before they exist. We know which shelf the stock is going to. We know what a full delivery should look like, so we can tell when it isn't. And we know which items need extra care: glass, liquids, anything with a cap.
>
> When the carton turns up, nobody is guessing. We're checking it against a list you already gave us.

## 2. Receiving and the photo report — 1:10–2:10
*Card: `02` · Receiving and the photo report*
*≈ 138 words · ≈ 57 s*

> This is the step I care about most.
>
> The carton goes onto the receiving table and gets opened. Everything comes out and is laid out in rows. Then the whole delivery is photographed in a single frame.
>
> Those photos go to you on the day the delivery is received. If something arrived crushed, short, or wrong, you see it that day. You find out about a problem before your customer does.
>
> There's a second reason we do it this way. When TikTok Shop looks into a delivery dispute, it asks for the item's condition at the time it was packed, and for proof that it was handed to the carrier. We collect exactly what's on that list, for every order.
>
> Whether the platform accepts it is the platform's decision. Our job is to make sure the evidence exists.

---

## 3. When something is wrong — 2:10–2:50
*Card: `03` · When something is wrong*
*≈ 96 words · ≈ 40 s*

> Three things can go wrong.
>
> Something is damaged. Something is missing. Or something arrived that wasn't on the list.
>
> Damaged stock does not go on a shelf. It goes into one box, marked damaged and on hold, so it can't be picked by mistake.
>
> Missing and extra items are counted against what you told us to expect.
>
> Then it's your decision whether you send it back to your supplier, write it off, or sell it as seconds. Those are your decisions, not ours. The warehouse records the problem and keeps the stock apart until you've decided.

---

## 4. Putaway and the stock record — 2:50–3:30
*Card: `04` · Putaway and the stock record*
*≈ 90 words · ≈ 37 s*

> Good stock goes to the shelves. Every product gets a location, A1 to A6 in this bay, and a quantity.
>
> From that point the system says what's on the shelf, and the shelf has to agree with it.
>
> That doesn't happen by trust. It happens by counting. We recount locations and compare the number on the shelf with the number in the record. If they don't match, we find out why before it turns into an order we can't fill.
>
> A stock figure is only useful if someone checked it.

---

## 5. The order arrives — 3:30–4:05
*Card: `05` · The order arrives*
*≈ 71 words · ≈ 29 s*

> Your customer buys on TikTok Shop or on your Shopify store. That order has to reach the warehouse, with the items and the address together, before anything can be picked.
>
> However it gets to us, the clock has already started. On TikTok Shop, the platform gives a seller 48 hours to ship an order. That's the platform's rule, not a promise of ours. It's the clock every order here starts on.

---

## 6. Picking — 4:05–4:35
*Card: `06` · Picking*
*≈ 68 words · ≈ 28 s*

> Picking goes by location, not by memory.
>
> The order says which product. The record says which shelf. We go to that address and take the item from that cell.
>
> It sounds slower than someone who just knows where everything is. It isn't, and it doesn't break when that person is off, or when two products look almost the same.
>
> An address is something you can check. Memory isn't.

---

## 7. Packing — 4:35–5:25
*Card: `07` · Packing*
*≈ 116 words · ≈ 48 s*

> Packaging is chosen by category, not by habit.
>
> A polybag for soft goods that can't break. A padded mailer for small things that need some cushioning. A box for glass, liquids and anything with a shape to protect.
>
> Size matters as much as protection. Couriers charge by dimensional weight: length times width times height, divided by five thousand. They bill whichever is bigger, that figure or the actual weight. So a light item in a box that's too big is paid for as a heavy one.
>
> For cosmetics the rules are specific: tape the cap and bag it, make sure glass can't move, and keep stock away from heat. There's a separate video on exactly that.

---

## 8. Label and dispatch — 5:25–6:00
*Card: `08` · Label and dispatch*
*≈ 81 words · ≈ 34 s*

> The parcel goes on the scale. The weight and the order produce the shipping label, and the label goes on the parcel.
>
> Finished parcels go into the dispatch cage, and the cage is handed to the carrier.
>
> The parcel leaves with a tracking number, and that number goes onto the order, so your customer can follow it.
>
> Every carrier has a daily collection cut-off. The warehouse plans the day backwards from it, so the packing is finished before the van arrives.

---
